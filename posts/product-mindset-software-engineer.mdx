If you are a beginner, or someone who wants to get into software engineering, take a minute to read this from a fellow SE intern who learned it the hard way.

In typical software engineering, engineers build software based on the given guidelines and follow a software development life cycle.

In this era of agentic coding, expectations have shifted too. Routine bug fixes and feature releases are becoming daily chores. The typical day of a software engineer has changed. My advice for anyone starting out in software engineering, or any other tech field, is to follow the product engineer mindset.

Product engineers are developers with a deep interest in the product itself. They understand their product in depth, they know its nooks and crannies well enough to market it, and they want to be involved in product decisions.

Here are a few traits of a product engineer worth studying.

**1. Don't settle for a single opinion or product idea.** Branch out and explore the possibilities. Challenge existing processes and products, and suggest alternative approaches.

**2. Have a real interest in the business and in how end users behave, based on the data.** You need enough statistical knowledge to understand how these functions and goals are actually being achieved — a data-driven approach rather than guessing or assuming.

**3. Always question how a product works.** Why is this successful? Why is this not working? Why should we have this feature now? The answers should come from data-backed choices.

**4. Build strong relationships with non-technical people.** Understand everyone involved in the product and what they do, communicate clearly with them, and learn how the other sections work.

**5. Look at all the possibilities.** In software engineering we call them edge cases. As a product engineer you should look at every possibility, find ways to reduce the work they create, and decide how to handle them.

**6. Repeat cycles of learning until you reach the right state.** Ask questions about a feature, bring suggestions and trade-offs, build it, get feedback, watch it closely while it's live, and compare expectations against actual results. If it didn't reach the goal, dig deeper, understand why, and learn from it. That is the product engineer's life cycle in summary.

These are some tips you can follow to become a better software engineer in 2026. Keep in mind that companies don't want a simple bug fixer or feature developer. They want someone who can grow the product with them, and who takes ownership.
